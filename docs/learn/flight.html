How a multirotor flies

Fundamentals, chapter 2

A multirotor has no wings, no rudder, and no moving surfaces. It does everything by changing the speed of its motors. This chapter shows how.

Thrust and weight

A propeller pushes air down. The air pushes back up with the same force. That upward force is thrust. When the thrust of all the propellers equals the weight, the drone hovers. More thrust and it climbs. Less and it sinks.

Three ways to rotate

A drone can turn about three axes. Pilots name them roll, pitch, and yaw.

Roll and pitch do more than tilt the drone. They are how it travels. A tilted drone points part of its thrust sideways, and that part pushes it along. Yaw only changes where the nose points.

Which motors speed up

To tilt, the flight controller speeds up the motors on one side and slows the ones on the other side. The total thrust stays about the same, so the height does not change.

How yaw works, with no tail

A spinning propeller tries to twist the drone the opposite way. That is why neighbouring propellers spin in opposite directions: two clockwise and two counter clockwise, so the twists cancel. To turn the nose, the flight controller speeds up one pair and slows the other pair. The twists no longer cancel, and the drone turns. The total thrust is unchanged, so it neither climbs nor sinks.

Try it in the trainer. Watch the four motor bars in the Instruments section while you fly. Push forward and the rear bars grow. Hold a yaw key and one diagonal pair grows. Lab 6 is a good place to look.

Forces in forward flight

Two things follow from this picture. First, when you tilt without adding throttle, the lift part shrinks and the drone sinks. Second, the faster you fly, or the stronger the headwind, the more tilt and thrust you need, and the faster the battery drains.

Try it in the trainer. Lab 12 lets you feel the sink when you tilt. Lab 19 shows what a headwind does to speed and effort.

Why it needs a computer

A multirotor is not stable by itself. Left alone, a small tilt grows until it flips. No person can correct four motors fast enough. The flight controller does it, in a loop that never stops.

Flight modes are levels of help

Each flight mode adds one more sensor to the loop, and one more job that the drone does for you.

StabilizedGyroscope, accelerometerKeeps itself level when you let goHeight, position, braking, wind

Position holdAdds GPS and compassAlso stops and stays in placeDeciding where to go

Camera drones fly in position hold nearly all the time. Learn the other two anyway. GPS can fail near buildings, under trees, or indoors, and then the drone drops to a simpler mode without asking.

Wind

To hold still in wind, the drone leans into it, just as in forward flight. Its speed over the ground is zero, but its speed through the air equals the wind speed. Wind is weaker near the ground and stronger at height, so a calm day at your feet can still be a windy day at 40 m.

Try it in the trainer. Lab 7 shows the lean. Lab 17 has you measure the wind at five heights.
