Parts of a multirotor

Fundamentals, chapter 1

A multirotor looks complicated, but it is a short list of parts, and each part has one job. This chapter names them, says what each one does, and shows how to recognize it.

How the parts work together

Your sticks send a command by radio. The flight controller reads that command and its own sensors, then decides how fast each motor should turn. The speed controllers carry out that decision. The battery feeds everything.

The airframe and the parts that make thrust

Frame

What it is. The skeleton: a center body and one arm for each motor. Usually carbon fiber or plastic.

What it does. Holds everything in place and keeps the motors the same distance apart. It must be stiff, because a frame that flexes shakes the sensors.

How to spot it. The X or H shape. Size is given as the distance between diagonal motors, for example 450 mm.

Brushless motor

What it is. An electric motor with no brushes to wear out. The outer can spins around a fixed core.

What it does. Turns the propeller. A rating called KV tells how fast it spins per volt. Low KV with a large propeller lifts weight. High KV with a small propeller gives speed.

How to spot it. A short metal cylinder at the end of each arm, with three wires.

Propeller

What it is. A small rotating wing. Marked with two numbers, such as 10 x 4.5: diameter and pitch, in inches.

What it does. Pushes air down, and the air pushes the drone up. Half of the propellers are made to spin clockwise and half counter clockwise. They are not interchangeable.

How to spot it. Two or three blades on top of each motor. A chipped propeller must be replaced, never flown.

Electronic speed controller (ESC)

What it is. A power switch that works thousands of times per second. One for each motor.

What it does. Takes a speed command from the flight controller and feeds the motor the right power from the battery.

How to spot it. A small flat board on the arm or inside the body. Two thick wires in from the battery, three out to the motor.

Landing gear

What it is. Legs or skids under the body.

What it does. Keeps the camera and the battery off the ground and takes the bump of landing.

How to spot it. Two skids, or a short leg under each arm.

The brain and its senses

Flight controller (FC)

What it is. A small computer board, often with an arrow that must point to the front.

What it does. Reads the sensors and your sticks hundreds of times each second and sets the speed of every motor. Without it a multirotor cannot fly at all.

How to spot it. A square board at the center of the frame, mounted on soft pads to keep vibration out.

IMU: gyroscope and accelerometer

What it is. Tiny motion sensors on the flight controller. IMU means inertial measurement unit.

What it does. The gyroscope feels how fast the drone is rotating. The accelerometer feels which way is down. Together they tell the drone its tilt.

How to spot it. You cannot see them. They are chips on the flight controller board.

Barometer

What it is. An air pressure sensor.

What it does. Air pressure falls as you go up, so it tells the drone its height. This is what makes altitude hold possible.

How to spot it. A tiny chip, often covered with a bit of foam so that propeller wash does not disturb it.

GPS and compass

What it is. A satellite receiver, usually paired with a magnetic compass.

What it does. GPS tells the drone where it is. The compass tells it which way it points. Together they make position hold, return to home, and planned missions possible.

How to spot it. A round puck, often on a short mast to keep it away from the magnetic field of the power wires.

Range and obstacle sensors

What it is. Ultrasonic, laser, or camera sensors that measure distance.

What it does. Pointing down, they give exact height near the ground for soft landings. Pointing outward, they detect obstacles.

How to spot it. Small round or rectangular windows on the belly and the sides.

Try it in the trainer. The trainer has all of these senses. Open the Sensors section in the side panel and watch "Range down", "Range front", and "Range up" change. Lab 8 uses them.

Power

Battery

What it is. A lithium polymer (LiPo) pack made of cells in series. Each cell is 3.7 V nominal and 4.2 V when full. "3S" means three cells, 11.1 V.

What it does. Stores the energy. Capacity is given in mAh. A larger pack flies longer but weighs more, so there is a best size for every drone.

How to spot it. The largest and heaviest single part, usually strapped on top or slid into the body.

Power distribution board

What it is. A board or wiring harness. On small drones it is built into the frame or the flight controller.

What it does. Splits battery power to the four speed controllers and gives clean low voltage to the electronics.

How to spot it. A flat board with thick solder pads near the center.

Battery safety. LiPo packs hold a lot of energy and can catch fire if damaged, overcharged, or run flat. Charge them on a proper balance charger, on a surface that cannot burn, and never unattended. Never use a swollen or punctured pack. Land before each cell drops below about 3.5 V. Store packs half charged, at about 3.8 V per cell.

Links and payload

Receiver

What it is. A small radio on the drone, paired to your transmitter. Pairing is called binding.

What it does. Turns the radio signal from your sticks into commands for the flight controller.

How to spot it. A thumbnail-sized box with one or two thin wire antennas. Keep the antennas apart and clear of carbon fiber.

Telemetry and video link

What it is. A second radio, or a part of the main one.

What it does. Sends data back to you: battery, height, position, and often live video. It also carries planned missions up to the drone.

How to spot it. A short stub antenna on the drone, and a screen, phone, or tablet on your side.

Camera and gimbal

What it is. The camera is the sensor you fly for. The gimbal is a motorized mount.

What it does. The gimbal keeps the camera level and steady while the drone tilts. For mapping, the camera points straight down.

How to spot it. Hangs under the nose or the belly.

Other payloads

What it is. Anything carried to do a job: a multispectral or thermal camera, a light sensor, a spray tank, a sampler.

What it does. Does the work. Every gram of payload costs flight time, which the design chapter explains.

How to spot it. Under the body, close to the center so the drone stays balanced.

The RC transmitter

The transmitter is the part you hold. It has two sticks, and each stick moves two ways, which gives the four basic commands. Most pilots use the layout called Mode 2, shown here. The trainer uses Mode 2 as well.

ControlWhat it does
Left stick, up and downThrottle: climb and descend. On many transmitters this stick does not spring back to center.
Left stick, left and rightYaw: turn the nose.
Right stick, up and downPitch: move forward and back.
Right stick, left and rightRoll: move left and right.
SwitchesFlight mode, return to home, camera, and arming. Each uses one more radio channel.
TrimsSmall buttons that shift the center of a stick. Rarely needed with a modern flight controller.

Words to know. A channel is one control the radio can carry. Four are the minimum, and six to eight are common. Binding pairs one transmitter with one receiver, on the 2.4 GHz band. Failsafe is what the drone does when it loses your signal. Set it to return to home or to land, and test it on the ground with the propellers off.

Try it in the trainer. A USB game controller or an RC transmitter in USB joystick mode works with the trainer. Open "Controller setup" in the side panel. Lab 1 is a good first flight with it.
