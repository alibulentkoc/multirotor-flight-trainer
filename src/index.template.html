Flight plan

North is up. H is the home point where you stand. With your own field loaded, "Set home" followed by a click on the map moves it. The yellow line is the track actually flown, and green dots are photos taken.

Move the right stick at any time to take over. At 20 % battery the drone abandons the plan and returns home.

Terms you need to know
AGL (above ground level)
Height measured from the ground directly below the drone. Flight plans and the 400 ft rule use AGL.
MSL (mean sea level) and elevation
Elevation is the height of the ground above sea level. Altitude MSL = field elevation + AGL. GPS and airspace charts report MSL. This simulator uses flat ground, so the two differ by a constant.
Terrain following
On sloped fields the planner adjusts each waypoint so AGL stays constant. Without it, GSD and overlap change as the ground rises and falls. Not simulated here.
GSD (ground sample distance)
The ground size of one pixel, in cm/pixel. GSD = sensor width x altitude / (focal length x image width). Lower altitude gives finer GSD but more photos.
Footprint
The patch of ground covered by one photo. It grows in direct proportion to altitude.
Front overlap (forward overlap, endlap)
Share of a photo that repeats in the next photo on the same flight line. It sets the distance between shutter triggers. 70 to 80 % is typical for crops.
Side overlap (sidelap)
Share of a photo that repeats in the neighbouring flight line. It sets the line spacing. 60 to 75 % is typical. Uniform canopies such as forage need more overlap, because stitching software finds fewer matching features.
Flight line (transect, leg)
One straight pass over the field. The back-and-forth set of lines is called a lawnmower or grid pattern.
Trigger distance and interval
Distance, and time, between photos. Interval = trigger distance / speed. If the interval is shorter than the camera can write images, slow down or fly higher.
Nadir
Camera pointing straight down. Mapping flights use nadir images.
Georeferenced image
An image that carries its own ground coordinates: a GeoTIFF, a KMZ image overlay, or a JPG with a world file. A plain screenshot does not, so you must give its scale.
KML and KMZ
Google Earth formats for points, paths, polygons, and image overlays. KMZ is a zipped KML that can also hold the image. Coordinates are longitude, latitude, altitude.
Waypoint
A position and altitude the autopilot flies to. A plan is an ordered list of waypoints.
Home point and RTH (return to home)
Where the drone took off. RTH brings it back on low battery, lost link, or pilot command.
VLOS (visual line of sight)
You must be able to see the aircraft with your own eyes through the whole flight. Check the pilot view during a plan to judge this.
400 ft ceiling
Under US Part 107 rules, small UAS stay at or below 400 ft (122 m) AGL unless near a taller structure.
Crosswind and crab
Wind across the flight line pushes the drone off track. The autopilot tilts into the wind to hold the line. Flying lines into and with the wind keeps the footprints square.
Orthomosaic and GCP
An orthomosaic is the single map stitched from the overlapping photos. Ground control points are surveyed markers that tie it to true coordinates.
